A design rig, not a second product. The same face the Sailfish app draws, in a browser, so the layout and the Fiat colours standard can be judged before anything is built. Swipe the rows, type in the field, throw the ambience switch.

The three gestures

  • Swipe rightComplete, behind the standard SFOS remorse timer. A recurring task says “Next time” instead of “Done”, because it is moved, not finished.
  • Swipe leftDelete. A real delete — there is no soft-delete behind it, and the remorse timer is the whole safety net.
  • HoldThe two date shortcuts: Not now and No date on a dated task, Due today and Due tomorrow on one without.
  • Drag the gripReorder — and the grip only appears in Anytime and inside a list, where order is manual. A date-sorted view makes no promise it cannot keep.
  • TapOpen the task: subtasks, list, recurrence, the optional time.

Capture inherits the view

Type in Today and the task is due today. Type in Upcoming and it lands tomorrow. Type inside a list and it joins that list. The placeholder says so out loud — “Add to today” — so it reads as a rule rather than as magic. No dialog stands between the thought and the row.

Plum, and what it may never mean

The accent is #6E4A63. It marks what is selected, what recurs, what is interactive — and nothing else. Green, amber and red are reserved for meaning: finished, due today, late. An app whose accent could be read as a verdict is an app that says different things in different ambiences.

Under an ambience, nothing is painted

Throw the switch and the gradient disappears entirely: the wallpaper becomes the background and every colour comes from Theme.*. The two ambience modes here stand in for that. The semantic three shift between a dark and a light variant to keep their contrast, and never take the accent's place.

A browser stand-in for qml/pages/MainPage.qml, TaskPage.qml and cover/CoverPage.qml. Behaviour is faithful; the real thing is Silica, and the remorse timer, the pull-down menu and the keyboard belong to the platform.
